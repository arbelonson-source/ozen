# Getting started with Ozen

For the person who will use Ozen, and anyone helping to set up the phone.

## What Ozen does

Ozen turns what the people around you say into large captions on your
phone, as they speak. It was made for someone who is deaf or hard of
hearing. The captions are always of Hebrew speech. The buttons and
settings can be in any of twelve languages. Once the speech model is on the
phone, the captions are written on the phone itself, with no internet.

## The first launch

The first time Ozen opens, it shows six short pages in large type. Swipe, or
tap **Next** (ha-ba) at the bottom. Tap **Skip** (dilug) to leave the
pages at any time. On the last page the button changes to **Start**
(lehatchil).

1. **Welcome.** Says what the app is. On a phone that keeps everything
   inside, it says that nothing is sent to the internet.
2. **How it works.** Four short lines: captions run on their own, the app
   tells speakers apart, it alerts you to names, doorbells and alarms, and you
   can type a reply that the phone says aloud.
3. **One-time setup.** You choose how the captions are written. See the
   next section.
4. **The microphone.** The first permission. See below.
5. **When you're called.** You type the name, and the phone then vibrates
   and marks the line whenever someone says it. See below.
6. **Ready.** Tells you what to expect on the first run. The button then
   starts the captions.

If a family member scanned the home computer's code first, the setup page
already shows the home computer as chosen. You can leave it that way.

### The speech model

On the setup page, **Accurate Hebrew (recommended)** (ivrit meduyeket,
mumlatz) is the usual choice. It downloads one file of about 819 MB, once.
Do this over Wi-Fi, because on mobile data a file this size can cost money or
use up the data plan. The page says it then takes a few minutes to set the
model up for the phone. After that it works without the internet.

- If the phone is short of room, the page says how much to free up.
- If the phone is old or slow, open **An older or slow phone?** (telefon
  yashan o iti?). There you can pick Fast instead. The page warns that it
  gets many more Hebrew words wrong, so use it only when the accurate one
  is too slow.
- **No download** (bli horada) uses Apple's own recognition, built into the
  phone. It starts right away, but Hebrew is only available on some
  versions of iOS.

You can change your mind at any time in Settings.
After you tap Start, the status button at the bottom of the caption screen
shows the download, for example "Downloading the language model · N%"
(morid et model ha-safa · N%). Keep the app open until it finishes. If the
phone is not on Wi-Fi, the button says "Waiting for Wi‑Fi to download the
language model" (mamtin le-Wi-Fi kdei lehorid et model ha-safa). The
download then starts by itself when Wi-Fi comes back. You can also tap the
button, and Ozen asks whether to download over mobile data now or to wait
for Wi-Fi.

### The microphone

Ozen needs the microphone to hear the conversation. Tap **Approve the
microphone** (le'asher et ha-mikrofon) and answer the question iOS shows.
When the audio stays on the phone, the page says that the sound doesn't
leave the phone and isn't kept unless you mark a problem.

If the answer is no, the page says the microphone is blocked and that
without it there are no captions. Tap **Open phone settings** (ptichat
hagdarot ha-telefon), turn the microphone on for Ozen, and come back. The
page checks again by itself when you return. If it is still off later, the
status button at the bottom of the caption screen says "No microphone
access" (ein gisha la-mikrofon). Tap it and it opens iOS Settings.

### Notifications

Once the microphone is approved, the same page offers **Approve
notifications** (le'asher hatra'ot). They let Ozen tell you about a
doorbell, an alarm or your name when the phone is in a pocket or locked. If
you say no, the page shows that there are no notifications and that this
can be changed in Settings. The captions still work.

### Speech recognition

Ozen does not ask for this permission during the pages. Only if you chose
Apple's own recognition, iOS may ask for permission to recognize speech
the first time the captions run. If it is refused, the status button says
"No speech recognition permission" (ein ishur le-zihuy dibur). Tap it to
open iOS Settings.

### Your name

The fifth page has a box, **Your name** (ha-shem shelcha), and a button,
**Add** (hosafa). Type the name as people say it, in the letters the captions
use. Hebrew letters are best, because captions are written in Hebrew and a
word in other letters may never be found. Later, Settings → Alerts →
Important words lets you add or remove words.

## The caption screen

The captions fill the screen, one line for each thing said, and the
newest at the bottom. A row of buttons runs along the bottom, from the first
button to the last:

- **Microphone** (mikrofon). Shows which microphone is in use, such as
  the phone's own, AirPods or a wired one. Tap it to choose another.
- The status button, in the middle. It says what Ozen is doing. While it says
  "Listening" (makshiv) and "Tap to pause" (hakishu kedei lehashhot), a tap
  pauses. When it says "Paused" (mushhe) and "Tap to continue" (hakishu kedei
  lehamshich), a tap goes on. When it says "Captions are off" (ha-ktuviyot
  kvuyot), a tap starts them. When the phone is saying a typed reply, a tap
  stops the phone talking.
- **Say** (lehagid). Opens the reply screen, described below.
- **Clear** (nikuy). Only shown when there are lines on the screen. It
  wipes the screen and starts fresh. If conversations are being saved, the
  lines go to History first. If not, Ozen asks before deleting them.
- **Settings** (hagdarot).

When the captions follow the newest line, the buttons slide away after a
few seconds so they never cover it. Touch the screen to bring them back.
To keep them on screen, turn off **Hide the buttons while captions are
running** (lehastir et ha-kaftorim bi-zman ha-azana) in Settings.

There is no separate stop button. To switch the captions off completely, use
Siri or a shortcut (see the lock screen section below).

### Text size

Spread two fingers apart on the captions to make the text bigger, and pinch
to make it smaller. A small label shows the size while you do it. Settings
also has **Text size** (godel text), with a preview sentence and a button to
go back to the usual size.

### Typing a reply

Tap **Say**. Type what you want to say in the box, then tap **Play**
(lehashmia). The phone says it aloud. While it talks, the captions pause, so
the phone does not caption itself, and they come back by themselves. Tap
**Stop** (atzira) to cut it off.

Under the box are **Quick phrases** (mishpatim muchanim), ready sentences you
can tap to say at once. **Edit** (aricha) lets you add phrases or restore
the original ones. If the phone has no voice for the language, the screen
says where to add one.

**Full screen, big letters** (masach male be-otiyot gdolot) opens a pad that
fills the whole screen with huge letters. Hand the phone to the other
person so they can write to you. It has **Clear** (nikuy), **Flip**
(lehafoch), which turns the text upside down for the person facing you,
**Speak** (lehashmia) and **Close** (sgira).

### Starring lines and naming speakers

Press and hold a line to open its menu:

- **Mark as important** (simun ke-chashuv) stars the line. Tap the same
  item again (it then says **Remove mark**, bitul ha-simun) to take the
  star off.
- **Ask them to repeat that** (levakesh she-yachzeru al ze) has the phone
  say that request aloud. It shows only when the phone can speak.
- **Who is speaking?** (mi medaber?) lets you type a name. From then on,
  everything that voice says shows with that name. If no voice has been
  identified for the line yet, try again after the person has talked a bit
  more.
- **Fix a word for next time** (tikun mila la-milon) teaches Ozen the right
  spelling of a word or name.
- **Copy** (ha'atakah) copies the line.
- **Captions not good? Mark a problem** (ha-ktuviyot lo tovot? lesamen
  be'aya) saves the details on the phone, so that whoever helps you can see
  them. See [Troubleshooting](troubleshooting.md).

Speaker names can be turned on or off with **Show speaker names** (lehatzig
shemot dovrim) in Settings.

### History

Settings → **Previous conversations** (shichot kodmot) opens the saved
conversations. You can search what was said, and delete one or all of them.
The list has a **Starred lines** (shurot mesumanot) entry that gathers
every line you starred. A switch there, **Save conversations** (lishmor
shichot), turns saving off, and an automatic deletion setting removes old
conversations. Conversations with a starred line or a name are always kept.
They stay on the phone, and in its own backup when the backup is on.

Near the bottom of Settings, **Show the initial walkthrough again**
(lehatzig shuv et ha-hesber ha-rishoni) brings back the six pages from the
first launch.

## The lock screen, Siri and the Action button

**Captions on the lock screen.** While captions run, the newest lines also
show on the lock screen, so they can be read without unlocking the phone.
This is on by default. The switch is **Captions on the lock screen too**
(ktuviyot gam be-masach ha-ne'ila) in Settings. If Live Activities are off
for Ozen in the iPhone's own settings, Ozen's Settings says so and offers a
button to open them. If iOS closes the app, the lock screen says "Captions aren’t updating. Open Ozen." (ha-ktuviyot lo
mitaadkenot. pitchu et Ozen) instead of showing an old sentence as new.

**Siri and Shortcuts.** Ozen understands, in Hebrew or in English, commands
such as "Hey Siri, start captions in Ozen" and "Hey Siri, stop captions in
Ozen". Another one has the phone say a sentence aloud, and Siri asks what
to say. Stopping saves the conversation in History. The commands also work
from the lock screen and in automations in the Shortcuts app. Settings has a
section with the same examples, called **Siri and shortcuts** (siri
ve-kitzurei derech).

**The Action button.** One of Ozen's shortcuts opens the big-letters pad
straight away, so someone can write to you when the captions cannot keep
up. On an iPhone with an Action button, the iPhone's own settings let you
give that button a shortcut. Pick the Ozen one for the big-letters pad.

**Control Center.** On iOS 18 or later, a **Start captions** button
(hatchalat ktuviyot) opens Ozen and starts listening in one press. To add
it, press and hold an empty spot in Control Center and search for Ozen. It
can also replace the flashlight or camera button at the bottom of the lock
screen.
